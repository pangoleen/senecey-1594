BnF, Cinq Cents de Colbert 33, ff. 528r-529v (Gallica btv1b10033958p, views 533, 534, 535)
Pelissier (agent of the duke of Mayenne) to cardinal de Joyeuse. Madrid, 13 February 1594. Original.

Conventions
  Plain type    = clear text of the letter (my reading of the italic hand; single pass).
  [[ ... ]]     = text in cipher, deciphered here (pass A; a blind pass B measures the error; see NOTES.md).
  [x]           = letters I supply (lost in the gutter of the binding, or left out by the writer).
  (?)           = doubt. The list of doubts is at the end.
  Word division, capitals, apostrophes and punctuation are mine. The spelling is the writer's.
  Cover names in the cipher (my identifications, from the sense): Rubi = the king of Spain;
  Hipolite = the duke of Guise; le Fin = Henri of Navarre; Safir = the Pope; Phare = probably the
  duke of Lorraine. "Monseigneur" = the duke of Mayenne (clear).

ENGLISH TRANSLATION

As you were told before, [[ we held to the proposal that the ministers of Rubi [Spain] made in
favour of Hipolite [Guise], and made ]] every possible request that he [the King] should declare
his intention [[ on it and provide the means and forces needed to make it succeed, to ]] his
satisfaction and for the safety of religion and the good and peace of France. But we must suppose
that one of his ministers had told him [[ that a certain person (name not read) had disclosed the
other proposals that were to ]] be made before we arrived here. So he [[ would not let himself be
understood in any way. He had ]] Don Juan de Idiáquez say that the way to provide quickly for the
good of affairs was [[ to hide nothing; and that, if my lord [Mayenne] had sent here only to learn
his intention on what was proposed for Hipolite, ]] there was little reason for it, because a
letter was enough, and it should not have kept him three or four months [[ from his decision,
which he would have taken if my lord had not begged him to delay until he had ]] sent to him. It
was a satisfaction that my lord owed him to let [[ Rubi choose one of the ways that he himself ]]
judged fit and reasonable. Idiáquez said that he had tried in all his actions to show his
affection for the Catholic cause and for my lord, and had drawn hatred, envy and slander on
himself for it; so he advised that [[ we go on to the other proposals, without which we would
take away nothing that could ]] give satisfaction. The true way to put some order in these affairs
was [[ to speak frankly. We answered that it is not my lord's intention to constrain or force the
will of Rubi, ]] but that it was not reasonable either to throw on my lord the hatred for the
displeasure [[ that Hipolite would feel if proposals were made (?) that passed over his own, ]] if
the King settled on another that pleased him; and that, when the King made himself understood on
it, he would be shown [[ that my lord wishes to comply with all that is possible for him with
honour, to please him, ]] provided he is shown clearly the means to [[ preserve religion and the
state whole, and that the consent and authority of Safir [the Pope] (?) come into the ]]
decisions that are taken. Don Juan de Idiáquez insisted that His Majesty [[ would take it ill if
we held out (?) longer, and that nothing else was to be expected from him until he had heard ]]
the rest. Because delay and doubt bring disfavour on the affairs of the party, and because
[[ without the help of Rubi it is impossible to prevent the establishment of the Fin [Navarre],
it was judged right to declare the proposals ]] that my lord ordered to be made.

First: since the King wished [[ to honour the house of Lorraine in general and Hipolite ]] in
particular so far as to have him [[ named by his ministers for king of France, by means of his
marriage with the Infanta his daughter (?), ]] my lord feels this honour with much obligation and
humbly begs him to [[ keep to this counsel, as ]] the one he thinks most fit to content [[ the
French and to keep ]] the house of Lorraine in union and concord. He promises to serve with all
his power [[ and to yield his own claim (?), provided Rubi will use ]] the forces and means that
are needed, as they were set out to him, for [[ the establishment of this kingship, to the ruin
and confusion ]] of the enemies.

Second: he declares his displeasure at [[ the refusal of the proposal made in favour of the
Infanta and the archduke Ernest. ]] The fault is not his, but [[ that of the ministers of Rubi,
who would not ]] take his advice to wait until [[ we had forces to support it; with that, he is
sure it would have been ]] accepted. The King can believe that this was his intention, for he
knows well that the more [[ the King was engaged in our affairs, the more freely he would ]]
spend his means. To confirm this: if the King does not intend [[ to carry out what was offered for
Hipolite, my lord would wish that Rubi accept the crown of France for himself and his successors,
make it inseparable from that of Spain, and bind his heir (?) and his states from now on to
uphold ]] the one crown and the other. All the difficulties that can arise in this were set out.
If, when he has weighed them, the King finds them too great and will not [[ settle on this
proposal, my lord begs him to consider that the refusal of the proposal for the Infanta with the
archduke Ernest, and what was since proposed in favour of Hipolite, made it ]] impossible, or at
least very difficult. Yet if the King judged in his prudence that [[ it could be taken up again,
and would bear the cost to reach it, my lord will serve in it faithfully, provided Hipolite is
satisfied by another alliance and reward that can make him forget the greatness ]] he hoped for.

If none of these ways seemed fit, then in my lord's opinion [[ Phare would be the man who could
most easily be called to this greatness, because, as head of his house (?), ]] he would be helped
without difficulty by all his kin and [[ be recognised among the French as a prince full of equity
and justice. They would accept him, provided Rubi ]] showed that he would support him. Also, since
he has [[ so large (?) a state that he would draw some ]] means from it, and help from his allies
and neighbours, who love him well, there would be [[ assurance of success in this undertaking.

If ]] for some reasons the King would not hear of this, I humbly beg him not to take it ill that
[[ with modesty my lord declares to him, as a last proposal, that ]] his labours, and the merit
he thinks he has earned [[ with France and with Rubi, whom he wishes to serve and obey ]] above
all things, should not wholly shut him out [[ from claiming this rank. If the King would so far
oblige him as to support him in it with the same forces and means that he ]] would use, and that
would be needed, if [[ he took a personal interest, my lord believes that (word not read) ]]
would consent willingly, because they are already used [[ to take all commands from him, and the
favours that depend on the crown of France from ]] his hand and bounty. He begs His Majesty to
believe that in [[ this request he is not driven by passion or boundless ambition, and ]] that he
will have no will but the King's; and, when it is strengthened by the authority of His Holiness
[[ and by the means needed to establish the decisions that are taken, ]] he will follow it point
by point and not go against it in any way.

After these [[ proposals, it was said that, if none of them pleased Rubi ]] with their
conditions, there seemed to be no other [[ remedy than to recognise the Fin and come to terms with
him under the authority of Safir, and to try, with (words not read), Rubi and all the powers, to
secure religion and the state (?) with him; ]] a thing that wise men without passion judge
possible. A way would even be found to satisfy the private interests of His Catholic Majesty, by
renewing the treaties of alliance between the crowns of France and Spain.

Today Don Juan de Idiáquez answered on all the above. His Majesty had seen everything and, after
much thought, judged it not reasonable to decide before he had conferred with His Holiness and
had his blessing on it, the more so because the two had until now been so united in one will that
they always had the same aim, and he wished to do the same in future; which was also what my lord
wished. Because these exchanges need time, His Majesty had sent several dispatches to Flanders so
that my lord should be helped with the forces and means that are there, to keep the party from
growing weaker and the enemy from any important progress. He will send again and give other
provisions and orders to hold out until the decisions are fully taken. Meanwhile you could be
told how affairs stand, so that, together with His Majesty's ambassador in Rome, you press His
Holiness jointly for the way that is judged most fit. This is all the progress that could be made
until now, [[ (words not read) the order that was reasonable for the satisfaction of Hipolite. ]]
Please add your prudent counsel to this account, to choose the road you judge best for the common
safety, and do us the kindness to let us know as soon as you can what you learn of the leanings
over there, to which we believe they will conform here. [[ It does not seem fitting that M.
d'Orbais (?) should have a part in the above; so we have asked ]] that the ambassador be told to
say nothing of it to him. Madrid, 13 February 1594.

See NOTES.md, section "ff. 528-529", for rates and the sign-level problems.
1. The cover names are identified from the sense only. Rubi: the period gloss on f. 528r reads
   "ruby". Hipolite: the period gloss reads "hipolite". Safir, Phare and le Fin have no gloss.
2. f. 528r lines 8-9: twelve signs after "sieur" give l-u-d-e-m-i-c-d-o-c-?-i. I cannot read a name.
3. f. 528r line 33: an insertion of about 28 small signs above the line. Only "on faisoit" and
   "d'ouvertu[r]es" are read.
4. "fortes" (for "forces"), "francuement" (for "franchement"), "grance" (for "France"),
   "assieter" (for "assister"), "nemble" (for "ne semble"): one sign in each word reads wrong.
   The cause can be my transcription or the writer.
5. The initial of "Hipolite" is written with three different signs (or is absent). Open.
6. About 40 line ends of f. 528v are in the gutter. One to three signs are lost at each. The
   letters in square brackets restore them by the sense. "son oi[r ...] cou[...] et [...]" and
   "plus me[...]" are not restored with confidence.
7. "sa fille", "ceder s[i]enn[e]", "sa mans[io]n", "si [gran]d", "liberaux", "seccg asseurs",
   "l'e[st]at", "g-u-a-e ...ant": not resolved. They are given as the signs read.
8. The clear text had one pass. The signature is not read with confidence.
